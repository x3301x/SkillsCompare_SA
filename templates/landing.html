{#- ============ HOW IT WORKS ============ -#}

How it works

Four steps from a need to a reply

  1. Describe the need

    Enter the skill you need, how many learners, where, and how you want it delivered. Add dates, credential requirements and budget when you enquire.

  2. Compare like with like

    Results use the same fields for every programme. A price appears only where the provider supplies one; otherwise you see Request Quote.

  3. Choose up to three, then enquire

    Put up to three programmes side by side, including whether each price is per learner or per cohort. Then send a consent-based enquiry to the provider you select.

  4. The provider replies to you

    Only that provider receives your contact details. They contact you and handle negotiation, contracting, invoicing and delivery. SkillsCompare SA takes no payment.

{#- ============ TRUST ============ -#}

Credential checks

“Verified” is a record, not a badge.

A SkillsCompare administrator must record the issuing authority, the method and the date checked before any credential shows as verified. A certificate uploaded by a provider is evidence to review. It is never proof by itself.

Price and quality are separate. Sorting by lowest published price is a convenience. It does not rank quality or suitability, and quote-only programmes have no confirmed price.

The four states you will see

  • {{ seal({'state': 'verified', 'label': 'Verified by SkillsCompare', 'kind': 'Admin check on record'}) }}

    An administrator checked the register or document and recorded the authority, method and date. You can read all three on the programme page.

  • {{ seal({'state': 'pending', 'label': 'Under review', 'kind': 'Evidence submitted'}) }}

    Evidence has been submitted and is waiting for an administrator. Treat it as unconfirmed.

  • {{ seal({'state': 'unverified', 'label': 'Unverified claim', 'kind': 'Provider-stated'}) }}

    The provider says it holds this credential. No check has been recorded.

  • {{ seal({'state': 'none', 'label': 'Non-accredited short course', 'kind': 'No accreditation claimed'}) }}

    The provider makes no accreditation claim. We show that plainly rather than hide it.

Four kinds of recognition, kept apart

Provider accreditation
The organisation is approved by a body such as QCTO or a SETA.
Programme accreditation
This specific qualification is accredited, not just the provider.
Institutional status
Registration as a university, TVET college or private college.
Professional recognition
Facilitators or content recognised by a professional body. It says nothing about the organisation’s accreditation.
